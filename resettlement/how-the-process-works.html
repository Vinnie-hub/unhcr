Understanding Resettlement

How the process works

From registration to arrival, resettlement can take months or years. Here are the main stages.

  1. RegistrationYou register with UNHCR or the host government and your details are recorded.
  2. Refugee statusYour claim is assessed (refugee status determination) if you are not already recognised.
  3. Needs assessmentUNHCR staff assess whether resettlement is the right solution and whether you meet submission criteria.
  4. ReferralIf suitable, your case is referred to a resettlement country, which may also receive cases through its own selection missions.
  5. Country decisionThe country interviews you, runs security and medical checks, and decides whether to accept you.
  6. Travel preparationCultural orientation, travel documents and health checks are arranged, typically with IOM.
  7. Arrival and integrationYou are received, housed and supported with language, schooling, health care and work.

How long does it take?

Timelines vary widely by country, case and security situation. Do not rely on anyone who promises a date.

Resettlement is always free. UNHCR does not charge fees, sell places or accept applications through agents. If someone asks you for money, they are not acting for UNHCR.
Keep your details current. Tell UNHCR if your phone number, address or family situation changes so that you can be reached.
